About Me

Hi, I'm Angel.

I'm an operations strategist and UC Berkeley Haas MBA candidate interested in how AI is changing the way complex organizations operate, make decisions, and scale.

Before business school, I spent six years in consulting with Deloitte and KPMG, helping organizations redesign operations across payments, banking, telecommunications, manufacturing, energy, and the public sector. My work ranged from improving transaction operations for digital payments platforms to redesigning procurement processes, strengthening operating models, and leading transformation initiatives across multiple African markets.

Across those experiences, I realized I was most energized by one type of problem: helping organizations untangle complexity. Whether the challenge was operational inefficiency, fragmented decision-making, or scaling across functions, I enjoyed understanding how a system worked, identifying where it broke down, and designing practical ways to improve it.

Today, I'm exploring how that work changes in the age of AI.

Rather than thinking about AI as another technology to adopt, I'm interested in how it reshapes operating models, decision-making, and execution. I'm particularly curious about the organizations building and operating critical infrastructure, from AI infrastructure and energy systems to payments, digital identity, and other industries where reliability, scale, and operational excellence matter.

At UC Berkeley Haas, I'm building on that foundation through coursework in operations, strategy, and AI, while working on projects that combine market strategy, operational thinking, and AI-enabled workflows. During my International Business Development project, I advised a multinational identity and payments company on its European market-entry strategy, using AI to accelerate research while keeping business judgment at the center of the work.

Long term, I hope to help organizations navigate large-scale operational change, combining operations strategy, AI transformation, and cross-functional execution to build businesses that are more resilient, more efficient, and positioned for lasting competitive advantage.

Outside Work

Outside work, I'm happiest exploring. Curiosity shapes how I spend my time, whether that's wandering through a new city, getting lost in an art museum, or painting abstract pieces at home. I'm fascinated by how cities work, from their architecture and public spaces to the cultures that shape them, which explains my habit of watching documentaries and YouTube deep dives on places around the world.

Travel is one of my favorite ways to learn. I've explored six African cities through work, along with New York and Paris. Paris changed how I travel, teaching me that the best discoveries often come without an itinerary. The Musée de l'Orangerie remains my favorite museum in the world, and standing before Monet's Water Lilies is still my favorite museum experience.

Creativity and movement keep me grounded. I enjoy abstract painting for the freedom of creating without a plan, and I stay active through Pilates, cycling, tennis, and badminton, the racket sport I play most whenever I can find a court.

About this site

My portfolio, and my thinking space.

It brings together my professional work, selected case studies, projects, and reflections on operations strategy, AI transformation, infrastructure, and business building. While my resume captures outcomes, this site explains how I approach problems, make decisions, and think through complex operational challenges.

I'll also use this space to document what I'm learning throughout my MBA at UC Berkeley Haas and Columbia Business School: classes and research, conferences, company visits, and conversations with operators, founders, and industry leaders. As my interests evolve, I hope these essays become a record of how my thinking develops alongside my work. Whether you're a founder, operator, consultant, researcher, student, or simply curious about how AI is changing organizations, I hope you find something here that is useful, or at least makes you think a little differently about how great operations are built.
